## Market & Industry Analysis
Bharti Airtel is a leading global telecommunications provider operating within the highly consolidated
Indian telecom services market. The company ranks as the second-largest mobile operator in India
and is among the top three globally by subscriber base [Source: https://www.airtel.in/about-
bharti/about-bharti-airtel, https://pestel-analysis.com/blogs/competitors/airtel]. The industry is
experiencing a multi-year growth cycle, driven by rising data consumption, 5G adoption, and a
national push towards digitalization, though it faces significant substitution risks from over-the-top
(OTT) services.
Industry Overview and Market Position
Bharti Airtel operates in the Telecom Services industry, which encompasses wireless (mobile) and
wireline (fixed-line) services, including voice, data, broadband, Digital TV (DTH), and enterprise
solutions such as cloud services, cybersecurity, and IoT [Source:
https://stockanalysis.com/quote/nse/BHARTIARTL/]. In the Indian market, wireless services constitute
the vast majority of the sector [Source:
https://www.bhartihexacom.in/docs/ipo/CRISIL_assessment_of_telecom_industry_in_india_19_1_2024.pdf].
The Indian telecom services market generated revenue of USD 145.16 billion in 2025, with another
source estimating the market at USD 153.83 billion for the same year [Source:
https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/telecom-services-market/india,
https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-telecom-market]. The sector's gross
revenue was reported at USD 43.42 billion in FY25, and the total telephone subscriber base in India
reached 1.21 billion by June 30, 2025 [Source: https://www.ibef.org/industry/telecommunications].
The market is projected to reach USD 217.6 billion by 2030, reflecting a forecasted CAGR of 8.5% from
2026 to 2030 [Source: https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/telecom-services-
market/india]. An alternative forecast predicts the market will reach USD 186.61 billion by 2030,
growing at a CAGR of 3.94% [Source: https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-
telecom-market].
Reflecting this industry-wide growth, India's quarterly telecom revenue nearly doubled in the five
years since September 2019, implying a 14% five-year industry revenue CAGR [Source:
https://telecom.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/industry/indian-telecom-industrys-revenue-
doubled-in-5-years-bharti-airtel-biggest-gainer-motilal-oswal/116649696]. In line with this trend,
Bharti Airtel's own revenue grew at a 14% CAGR in the three years leading up to FY25 [Source:
https://www.airtel.in/airtel-annual-report-2024-25/pdf/26_Airtel_IAR_2024-
25_Management%20Discussion%20&%20Analysis.pdf].

25/pdf/26_Airtel_IAR_2024-25_Management%20Discussion%20&%20Analysis.pdf].

Industry Structure and Risks
The Indian telecom market is highly consolidated, with Bharti Airtel and two other major operators
accounting for approximately 92% of total wireless customers and 95% of the industry's wireless
revenue as of H1 fiscal 2024 [Source:
https://www.bhartihexacom.in/docs/ipo/CRISIL_assessment_of_telecom_industry_in_india_19_1_2024.pdf].
Within this structure, Bharti Airtel holds a wireless subscriber market share of around 34% [Source:
https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/regulatory/article/-/view/type/HTML/id/3479751].

- Barriers to Entry: The industry is characterized by very high barriers to entry.
- High Capital Intensity: Significant investment is required for network infrastructure, deployment, and
maintenance [Source: https://matrixbcg.com/products/airtel-five-forces-analysis].
- Regulated Environment & Spectrum Costs: The sector is highly regulated by the Department of
Telecommunications (DoT) and the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI). Spectrum, a critical
resource, must be acquired through expensive government auctions [Source:
https://matrixbcg.com/products/airtel-five-forces-analysis].
- Dominance of Existing Players: Established operators have extensive network coverage and large
customer bases, creating significant hurdles for new entrants [Source:
https://matrixbcg.com/products/airtel-five-forces-analysis].
- Economies of Scale: Achieving large scale is essential for profitability, particularly given the
relatively low ARPU in India compared to global markets [Source:
https://matrixbcg.com/products/airtel-five-forces-analysis].
## * Substitution Risks:
- OTT Communication: Applications like WhatsApp, Zoom, and Google Meet offer free or low-cost
voice, video, and messaging services, which directly substitute Bharti Airtel's traditional revenue
streams [Source: https://matrixbcg.com/products/airtel-five-forces-analysis].
- OTT Content Streaming: Services such as Netflix and Amazon Prime Video are major substitutes for
Bharti Airtel's DTH television business [Source: https://matrixbcg.com/products/airtel-five-forces-
analysis].
- Public Wi-Fi: The increasing availability of public and private Wi-Fi networks serves as a substitute
for mobile data usage [Source: https://matrixbcg.com/products/airtel-five-forces-analysis].
